# Personas and Jobs-to-be-Done

Personas and jobs-to-be-done (JTBD) are tools for understanding users. Personas describe who users are. JTBD describes what they are trying to achieve.

---

## 1. Personas

A persona is a fictional character that represents a group of users. It helps teams keep real users in mind.

### What a persona includes

| Section | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Name and photo** | “Sara, project manager.” |
| **Demographics** | Age, role, location, experience. |
| **Goals** | What they want to achieve. |
| **Frustrations** | What gets in their way. |
| **Behaviors** | How they currently work. |
| **Tools** | What they already use. |
| **Quote** | A sentence that captures their mindset. |

### Types of personas

- **Primary persona**: the main user the product is for.
- **Secondary persona**: also served, but not the primary focus.
- **Negative persona**: users the product is not for.
- **Anti-persona**: users to avoid designing for.

### When personas are useful

- The product has distinct user groups.
- The team needs a shared understanding of users.
- Design decisions need a clear reference point.

### When personas are not useful

- The audience is too broad.
- They are built on assumptions, not research.
- They become stereotypes.

---

## 2. Jobs-to-be-Done

JTBD focuses on the progress a user is trying to make in a given situation.

### Core idea

People do not buy products; they hire them to make progress.

### The JTBD statement

```
When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].
```

Example:

```
When I have five minutes before a meeting, I want to see my team’s progress quickly, so I can address blockers without reading every channel.
```

### Forces of progress

| Force | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **Push** | The problem that pushes the user to act. |
| **Pull** | The attraction of the new solution. |
| **Anxiety** | Fear that the new solution will not work. |
| **Habit** | The pull of the current way of doing things. |

To switch, the push and pull must overcome anxiety and habit.

---

## 3. Personas vs. JTBD

| Personas | JTBD |
|----------|------|
| Focus on who. | Focus on what and why. |
| Stable over time. | Situational and context-specific. |
| Good for empathy. | Good for prioritization and innovation. |
| Can become stereotypes. | Less prone to demographic bias. |

Both are stronger together. Personas provide the human context. JTBD provides the motivation.

---

## 4. Using Personas and JTBD in Design

1. **Frame problems around a persona’s goals.**
2. **Write JTBD statements for key moments.**
3. **Map solutions to jobs, not just personas.**
4. **Test whether the product helps users make progress.**
5. **Prioritize jobs by frequency, importance, and satisfaction gap.**

---

## 5. Common Mistakes

- Personas based on demographics alone.
- Too many personas.
- Personas that are not updated.
- JTBD that are too vague.
- JTBD that describe the product, not the user’s goal.
- Using personas without research.
- Confusing roles with jobs.

---

## 6. Checklist

- [ ] Personas are based on research, not assumptions.
- [ ] Personas include goals, frustrations, behaviors, and tools.
- [ ] Primary and secondary personas are clear.
- [ ] Personas are used to guide design decisions.
- [ ] JTBD statements are written for key situations.
- [ ] JTBD focus on progress, not features.
- [ ] Forces of progress are considered.
- [ ] Personas and JTBD are updated as the product evolves.
- [ ] Team can reference personas and jobs in discussions.
