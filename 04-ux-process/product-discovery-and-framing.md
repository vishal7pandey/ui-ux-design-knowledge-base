# Product Discovery and Framing

Product discovery is the work of understanding the right problem to solve before designing a solution. Framing is how the team agrees on what they are building and why.

---

## 1. Why Discovery Matters

Building the wrong thing well is a waste. Discovery helps teams:

- Understand user needs, pain points, and goals.
- Validate assumptions with evidence.
- Align on the problem before the solution.
- Reduce risk before investing in delivery.

---

## 2. Discovery Inputs

- **User research**: interviews, observations, surveys, support tickets.
- **Analytics**: usage, drop-off, conversion, feature adoption.
- **Business goals**: revenue, retention, efficiency, expansion.
- **Market and competitive context**: what others do and where the gaps are.
- **Technical constraints**: what is feasible and how fast.

---

## 3. Problem Framing

### The problem statement

A good problem statement is:

- Based on user needs, not feature ideas.
- Specific enough to be actionable.
- Broad enough to allow multiple solutions.
- Testable and measurable.

### Template

```
[User] needs to [job or goal] because [problem or pain point], which currently causes [impact].
```

Example:

```
Project managers need to share weekly updates because stakeholders lose context, which causes delays and repeated meetings.
```

### Jobs-to-be-Done

Ask: what job is the user hiring this product to do?

| Job statement | Example |
|---------------|---------|
| Functional | “Help me track my team’s progress.” |
| Emotional | “Help me feel in control of my work.” |
| Social | “Help me look good to my manager.” |

---

## 4. Discovery Activities

### User interviews

- Talk to target users about their workflows and problems.
- Ask open-ended questions.
- Look for patterns, not just quotes.

### Journey mapping

- Map the current experience step by step.
- Identify pain points and opportunities.
- Highlight moments of high emotion or friction.

### Assumption mapping

- List what the team believes to be true.
- Rate each by risk and uncertainty.
- Prioritize testing the riskiest assumptions.

### Prototype testing

- Test lightweight concepts with real users.
- Iterate before committing to build.

### Competitive analysis

- Study how others solve similar problems.
- Identify conventions and opportunities.

---

## 5. Framing the Work

### Product brief

A one-page document that captures:

- Problem
- Target users
- Goals and success metrics
- Constraints
- Hypothesis
- Open questions

### Opportunity solution tree

- **Goal** at the top.
- **Opportunities** that could help achieve it.
- **Solutions** for each opportunity.
- **Experiments** to test solutions.

### North Star

- A single, meaningful metric that reflects long-term value.
- Helps teams prioritize and align.

---

## 6. From Discovery to Design

1. **Frame the problem.**
2. **Generate solutions.**
3. **Prioritize.**
4. **Prototype and test.**
5. **Refine the solution.**
6. **Define scope and success metrics.**
7. **Hand off to delivery.**

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

- Starting with a solution instead of a problem.
- Skipping user research.
- Asking users what they want instead of observing what they do.
- Building for the “average” user.
- Ignoring technical or business constraints.
- Over-researching and under-shipping.
- Not writing down the problem statement.

---

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Problem is framed with user and business context.
- [ ] Assumptions are identified and prioritized.
- [ ] User research is conducted with real people.
- [ ] Journey or workflow is mapped.
- [ ] Opportunities are explored before solutions.
- [ ] Prototypes are tested and iterated.
- [ ] Success metrics are defined.
- [ ] Product brief is shared and understood.
- [ ] North Star or goal is clear.
- [ ] Team is aligned before delivery starts.
