# Content Strategy and Information Architecture

Content and structure are inseparable. Good information architecture (IA) helps users find what they need. Good content strategy makes sure what they find is useful.

---

## 1. Information Architecture

IA is the organization, labeling, navigation, and search that help users find and understand content.

### Core systems

| System | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Organization** | How content is grouped and categorized. |
| **Labeling** | How content is named. |
| **Navigation** | How users move through content. |
| **Search** | How users find content directly. |

---

## 2. IA Principles

- **Build for the user’s mental model**, not the company’s org chart.
- **Keep hierarchy shallow.** Two levels is enough for most products.
- **Use clear, consistent labels.** The same term should mean the same thing.
- **Provide multiple paths.** Search, navigation, and cross-links help users find content.
- **Test with real users.** Card sorting and tree testing reveal mismatches.

---

## 3. IA Methods

### Card sorting

- Users organize topics into groups that make sense to them.
- Helps discover natural categories.

### Tree testing

- Users find items in a proposed structure.
- Tests whether the IA is findable.

### Content audit

- Inventory all existing content.
- Identify duplicates, gaps, and outdated material.

### Sitemap

- A visual map of pages and their hierarchy.
- Used for planning and communication.

---

## 4. Content Strategy

Content strategy defines what content is needed, why, and how it is created, maintained, and measured.

### Content strategy pillars

| Pillar | Question |
|--------|----------|
| **Substance** | What content do we need? |
| **Structure** | How is it organized and connected? |
| **Workflow** | Who creates and maintains it? |
| **Governance** | Who decides and enforces standards? |

---

## 5. Content Principles

- **Purposeful.** Every piece of content has a clear job.
- **User-centered.** Content is written for the reader, not the writer.
- **Clear and concise.** Remove unnecessary words.
- **Consistent.** Terminology, tone, and style are unified.
- **Findable.** Content is organized and labeled for discovery.
- **Maintainable.** Content owners and update cadences are assigned.

---

## 6. Content Types

| Type | Use case |
|------|----------|
| **Microcopy** | Buttons, labels, error messages. |
| **Short-form** | Notifications, banners, descriptions. |
| **Long-form** | Help articles, blog posts, documentation. |
| **Visual** | Images, icons, videos, diagrams. |
| **Data** | Metrics, reports, records. |

---

## 7. Content Maintenance

- Schedule regular content audits.
- Remove or update outdated content.
- Assign owners to each content area.
- Track content performance.
- Plan for new content creation.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

- Organizing by internal team names.
- Using jargon or brand-specific terms users do not know.
- Too many levels of navigation.
- Outdated or duplicate content.
- No clear content ownership.
- Copy that is clear to the writer but not the user.
- No search or poor search.

---

## 9. Checklist

- [ ] IA is based on user mental models.
- [ ] Navigation is shallow and clear.
- [ ] Labels are consistent and plain.
- [ ] Search and navigation complement each other.
- [ ] Card sorting and tree testing have been done.
- [ ] Content has a clear purpose and owner.
- [ ] Tone and terminology are consistent.
- [ ] Outdated and duplicate content is removed.
- [ ] Content strategy includes workflow and governance.
- [ ] Content is measured and improved over time.
