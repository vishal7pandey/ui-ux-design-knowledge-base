# Service Design and Ecosystem Thinking

Service design looks at the whole experience: people, processes, touchpoints, and systems. Ecosystem thinking extends that to how the product connects with other products, services, and contexts.

---

## 1. What Is Service Design?

Service design is the practice of planning and organizing people, infrastructure, communication, and material components of a service to improve its quality and the interaction between the service provider and its users.

### Key areas

- **Users**: customers, employees, partners.
- **Touchpoints**: app, website, physical space, phone, email.
- **Processes**: how work gets done behind the scenes.
- **Systems**: tools, data, and infrastructure.

---

## 2. Service Design Principles

### Holistic

Look at the full journey, not just the digital interface.

### User-centered

Understand all users, including employees and partners.

### Cohesive

Make each touchpoint feel like part of one service.

### Iterative

Improve continuously based on feedback.

### Collaborative

Bring together design, business, operations, and technology.

---

## 3. Service Blueprints

A service blueprint maps the steps, touchpoints, and supporting processes of a service.

### Layers

1. **User actions**: what the user does.
2. **Frontstage actions**: what employees or the interface do.
3. **Backstage actions**: what happens behind the scenes.
4. **Support processes**: systems and tools that enable the service.

---

## 4. Ecosystem Thinking

Ecosystem thinking is about how the product fits into a larger network.

### Questions to ask

- What other products and services do users use?
- How does data flow between them?
- Where does the product start and end?
- What are the handoffs between systems and teams?
- What happens before, during, and after the user uses the product?

---

## 5. Ecosystem Mapping

An ecosystem map shows:

- Products and services.
- Users and roles.
- Data and content flows.
- Partnerships and integrations.
- Pain points and opportunities.

---

## 6. Designing for the Whole Experience

- Do not optimize one touchpoint at the expense of another.
- Consider the emotional arc across the whole journey.
- Design for users who may never see the interface.
- Plan for employees and support staff.
- Align business, technology, and operations.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

- Designing only for the digital interface.
- Ignoring the offline or human parts of the service.
- Not involving operations and support.
- Missing handoffs between touchpoints.
- No feedback loop between frontstage and backstage.
- Optimizing locally, not for the whole system.

---

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] The full user journey is mapped.
- [ ] All users, including employees, are considered.
- [ ] Service blueprint or map exists.
- [ ] Frontstage and backstage are aligned.
- [ ] Ecosystem map shows products, users, and data flows.
- [ ] Touchpoints are consistent and cohesive.
- [ ] Feedback loops exist between touchpoints.
- [ ] Business, operations, and technology are aligned.
- [ ] Handoffs are designed and tested.
- [ ] The service is improved continuously.
