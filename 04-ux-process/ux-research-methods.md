# UX Research Methods

Research is how we move from assumptions to evidence. The right method depends on the question, the stage of the project, and the resources available.

---

## 1. When to Use Research

| Project stage | Research goal | Typical methods |
|---------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Discovery** | Understand the problem and users | Interviews, field studies, diary studies, competitive analysis |
| **Define** | Synthesize insights and prioritize | Personas, journey maps, jobs-to-be-done, card sorting |
| **Design** | Explore and validate concepts | Wireframe testing, prototype testing, co-design, tree testing |
| **Build** | Catch issues before launch | Usability testing, accessibility audits, QA testing |
| **Launch** | Measure impact and iterate | A/B testing, analytics, surveys, support analysis |

---

## 2. Qualitative Methods

### User interviews

One-on-one conversations to understand motivations, behaviors, and pain points.

**Best for**: deep insights, early discovery, understanding why.
**Sample size**: 5–10 participants per segment.

### Contextual inquiry

Observe users in their real environment while they perform tasks.

**Best for**: understanding workflows, environmental constraints, unspoken needs.

### Diary studies

Participants record their experiences over time.

**Best for**: habits, longitudinal behavior, real-world friction.

### Focus groups

Guided group discussion.

**Best for**: exploring attitudes, generating ideas.
**Avoid for**: usability evaluation (group dynamics distort individual behavior).

---

## 3. Quantitative Methods

### Surveys

Structured questions answered by many users.

**Best for**: measuring attitudes, priorities, and demographics at scale.
**Watch out for**: leading questions, low response rates, self-reported bias.

### Analytics

Analyze behavioral data from the product.

**Best for**: understanding what users actually do, not what they say they do.

### A/B testing

Compare two or more versions to see which performs better.

**Best for**: validating design decisions, optimizing conversions.

### Card sorting

Participants organize topics into groups they understand.

**Best for**: information architecture, navigation labeling.

### Tree testing

Participants find items in a proposed IA.

**Best for**: validating navigation structure before building it.

---

## 4. Evaluative Methods

### Usability testing

Observe users completing tasks with a prototype or product.

**Best for**: finding usability problems and measuring task success.
**Sample size**: 5 users find ~85% of issues in a given task.

### Heuristic evaluation

Experts review the interface against established principles.

**Best for**: quick, cheap identification of obvious issues.

### Cognitive walkthrough

Step through each action a user takes to complete a task.

**Best for**: evaluating learnability and first-time use.

### First-click testing

Where do users click first to complete a task?

**Best for**: navigation and visual hierarchy validation.

### Accessibility audits

Evaluate against WCAG and real assistive technology use.

**Best for**: catching barriers for users with disabilities.

---

## 5. Generative Methods

### Workshops

Collaborative sessions to generate ideas and align teams.

**Best for**: ideation, journey mapping, prioritization.

### Co-design

Users participate in designing solutions.

**Best for**: inclusive design, novel solutions, user ownership.

### Competitive analysis

Study how other products solve similar problems.

**Best for**: identifying patterns, gaps, and opportunities.

---

## 6. Recruiting and Ethics

- Recruit participants who represent real user diversity.
- Provide informed consent and explain how data will be used.
- Protect privacy and anonymity.
- Compensate participants for their time.
- Avoid leading questions and avoid making participants feel judged.

---

## 7. Mixed-Methods Approach

Combine qualitative and quantitative methods:

1. **Start broad**: surveys, analytics, interviews to understand the landscape.
2. **Go deep**: interviews, contextual inquiry to understand motivations.
3. **Generate**: workshops, co-design to explore solutions.
4. **Validate**: usability testing, A/B testing to confirm what works.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

- Researching only once, not continuously.
- Asking users what they want instead of observing what they do.
- Testing only with ideal users.
- Ignoring qualitative insights in favor of numbers.
- Using the wrong method for the question.
- Not acting on research findings.

---

## 9. Research Checklist

- [ ] Research question is clear.
- [ ] Method matches the question.
- [ ] Participants represent real users and contexts.
- [ ] Consent and privacy are handled.
- [ ] Findings are synthesized and shared.
- [ ] Insights inform design decisions.
- [ ] Research is conducted continuously, not once.
