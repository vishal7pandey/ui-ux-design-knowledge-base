# Design Ops

Design Ops is the practice of improving the efficiency, quality, and impact of a design team. It covers tools, processes, people, and the bridge between design and the rest of the organization.

---

## 1. What Design Ops Covers

| Area | Focus |
|------|-------|
| **Tools** | Design, research, handoff, and collaboration tools. |
| **Workflow** | How design work moves from idea to ship. |
| **Governance** | Standards, reviews, and contribution models. |
| **Team health** | Hiring, onboarding, growth, and communication. |
| **Impact** | Measuring the value of design. |

---

## 2. Tooling Strategy

- Standardize on a small set of tools.
- Ensure design and engineering share a source of truth for components and tokens.
- Integrate design files with project management and handoff tools.
- Regularly review tool usage and cost.
- Provide training so the team uses tools well.

---

## 3. Workflow

### Design process

A clear, repeatable process helps the team move fast without missing important steps.

1. **Discover** — research, understand the problem.
2. **Define** — scope, goals, success metrics.
3. **Design** — explore, test, refine.
4. **Deliver** — handoff, build, QA.
5. **Measure** — track outcomes and iterate.

### Rituals

| Ritual | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Design critiques** | Improve work and align the team. |
| **Sprint planning** | Prioritize design work. |
| **Standups** | Share progress and blockers. |
| **Design reviews** | Get stakeholder approval. |
| **Retrospectives** | Reflect and improve the process. |

---

## 4. Design Quality

- Maintain a design system and component library.
- Define and document design principles, patterns, and heuristics.
- Run design QA before release.
- Include accessibility and inclusive design in every review.
- Track design-debt and schedule time to address it.

---

## 5. Collaboration with Engineering and Product

- Designers, engineers, and product managers should work as a trio.
- Involve engineering early, not just at handoff.
- Share design rationale in written form.
- Use the same language: user stories, acceptance criteria, tokens, components.
- Co-create and co-review work to reduce friction.

---

## 6. Measuring Design Impact

| Metric | What it shows |
|--------|---------------|
| **Time to design** | How fast a feature moves from concept to handoff. |
| **Time to ship** | How fast a design goes from concept to production. |
| **Design system adoption** | How much of the product uses shared patterns. |
| **Usability test pass rate** | Whether users can complete tasks. |
| **Error rate** | Where users fail or make mistakes. |
| **NPS / CSAT** | User satisfaction with the product. |
| **Design-to-code fidelity** | How closely the build matches the design. |

---

## 7. Team Health

- Onboard new designers with a clear guide and buddy.
- Define career paths and growth expectations.
- Protect focused design time; reduce unnecessary meetings.
- Foster a culture of feedback and critique.
- Encourage skill growth in research, systems, content, and code.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

- Buying tools without a process in place.
- Adding more designers without fixing the workflow.
- Letting design debt grow unchecked.
- Measuring output (screens) instead of outcomes (user success).
- Isolating design from product and engineering.

---

## 9. Checklist

- [ ] Tooling is standardized and integrated.
- [ ] Design process is documented and followed.
- [ ] Critiques, reviews, and QA are regular.
- [ ] Design system is maintained and adopted.
- [ ] Accessibility and inclusivity are part of every review.
- [ ] Design, product, and engineering collaborate continuously.
- [ ] Impact is measured with real outcomes.
- [ ] Designers have clear growth paths and onboarding.
- [ ] Design debt is tracked and addressed.
