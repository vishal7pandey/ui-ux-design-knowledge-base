# AI in Legal and Compliance UX

AI in legal and compliance helps review, analyze, and manage documents and risk. The UX must prioritize accuracy, auditability, and human oversight.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Accuracy

- Legal content must be precise.
- AI should not create false or misleading claims.
- Always provide sources and citations.

### Human oversight

- AI can assist, not replace, legal professionals.
- Final decisions and advice come from qualified humans.
- High-stakes actions require review.

### Auditability

- Every AI output and decision should be traceable.
- Keep records of documents, prompts, and changes.
- Support compliance and discovery.

### Security and confidentiality

- Legal data is highly sensitive.
- Strong encryption, access controls, and privacy are essential.
- Client-attorney privilege must be protected.

### Explainability

- Users need to understand why the AI made a finding.
- Cite clauses, cases, and documents.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Contract review and analysis.
- Legal research and case law search.
- Compliance document review.
- Regulatory monitoring.
- Due diligence.
- Discovery and e-discovery.
- Clause extraction and comparison.
- Risk assessment.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Document analysis

- Upload or select a document.
- AI identifies clauses, risks, and key terms.
- Show findings with citations and highlights.

### Contract review

- Flag unusual or risky terms.
- Compare to standard templates or past contracts.
- Show confidence and reasoning.

### Legal research

- Ask a legal question.
- AI returns relevant cases, statutes, and summaries.
- Link to full sources.

### Compliance checklists

- Track requirements and evidence.
- Show what is complete, missing, or at risk.
- Suggest remediation.

### Redlining and comparison

- Compare versions of a document.
- Highlight changes and potential issues.
- Let users accept or reject.

### Audit and report

- Generate audit logs and reports.
- Show what was reviewed and by whom.
- Export for compliance.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Always cite sources.
- Show confidence and uncertainty.
- Provide human review for high-risk findings.
- Keep audit trails.
- Protect client data and privilege.
- Use plain language for non-legal users.
- Allow users to verify and override AI.
- Update models with current law and regulation.
- Test for accuracy and bias.

---

## 5. Disclaimers

- “This is not legal advice.”
- “Generated output should be reviewed by a qualified attorney.”
- “AI analysis is for informational purposes only.”

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Providing legal advice without a qualified professional.
- Hallucinating cases or statutes.
- Not citing sources.
- No audit trail.
- Weak security or data protection.
- Over-automating high-stakes decisions.
- Inaccurate or outdated legal information.
- Not respecting client confidentiality.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] AI findings are cited and verifiable.
- [ ] Sources and confidence are shown.
- [ ] Disclaimers are clear and prominent.
- [ ] Human review is required for high-risk actions.
- [ ] Audit trails and logs are maintained.
- [ ] Data is secure and client-privilege protected.
- [ ] Compliance requirements are tracked.
- [ ] Documents can be compared and redlined.
- [ ] Reports are exportable.
- [ ] Accuracy and bias are tested.
- [ ] Updates reflect current law and regulation.
