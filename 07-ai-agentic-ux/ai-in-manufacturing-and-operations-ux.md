# AI in Manufacturing and Operations UX

AI in manufacturing and operations improves efficiency, safety, and quality. The UX must support workers on the floor, supervisors, and systems with clear, actionable information.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Safety first

- AI should not put workers or facilities at risk.
- Warnings and alerts must be clear and timely.

### Actionable insights

- Workers need clear, relevant information.
- Avoid overwhelming dashboards.

### Real-time

- Manufacturing moves fast.
- Updates and alerts must be near real-time.

### Reliability

- Downtime is expensive.
- AI predictions and recommendations must be accurate.

### Human-in-the-loop

- Workers and supervisors make final decisions.
- AI supports, not overrides, judgment.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Predictive maintenance.
- Quality inspection and defect detection.
- Production optimization.
- Supply and inventory monitoring.
- Safety and hazard detection.
- Worker guidance and training.
- Energy and resource management.
- Demand forecasting.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Operations dashboard

- Show key metrics: uptime, output, quality, safety.
- Highlight anomalies and alerts.
- Provide drill-down for details.

### Predictive maintenance

- Alert before equipment fails.
- Show risk level, recommended action, and timing.
- Allow scheduling maintenance.

### Quality control

- Flag defective items.
- Show confidence and image evidence.
- Let operators confirm or override.

### Worker guidance

- Step-by-step instructions on a screen or wearable.
- Use visuals and simple language.
- Adapt to the worker’s role and skill.

### Safety alerts

- Immediate, high-visibility warnings.
- Explain the hazard and required action.
- Confirm the worker has seen it.

### Production scheduling

- Optimize schedules based on demand and capacity.
- Show recommended changes.
- Let supervisors approve.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Design for noisy, hands-busy environments.
- Use large, clear visuals and minimal text.
- Prioritize alerts to avoid alarm fatigue.
- Make data accessible on mobile and wearables.
- Support offline use for floor systems.
- Provide historical context for predictions.
- Train workers on AI tools.
- Integrate with existing MES, ERP, and SCADA systems.
- Test in real production settings.

---

## 5. Privacy and Safety

- Protect worker data and location.
- Do not use AI for punitive monitoring.
- Follow safety and labor regulations.
- Provide clear escalation paths.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Too many alerts leading to alarm fatigue.
- Dashboards with too much data.
- AI overriding worker judgment.
- Slow or stale data.
- Not testing in real environments.
- Ignoring worker feedback.
- Using AI for surveillance rather than support.
- No integration with existing systems.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Dashboards show the most important metrics.
- [ ] Alerts are prioritized and actionable.
- [ ] Predictive maintenance is clear and timely.
- [ ] Quality control provides evidence and confidence.
- [ ] Worker guidance is visual and simple.
- [ ] Safety warnings are high-visibility and immediate.
- [ ] Schedules can be reviewed and approved by supervisors.
- [ ] Data is available on floor devices and wearables.
- [ ] Offline and poor-network modes are supported.
- [ ] Worker privacy and safety are protected.
- [ ] Integration with MES, ERP, and SCADA is provided.
- [ ] Tools are tested in real manufacturing settings.
