# Consent and Control in AI

Users need clear, meaningful control over what AI can access and do. Consent in AI is not a one-time checkbox; it is an ongoing agreement that users can understand and change.

---

## 1. Consent Principles

### Informed

Users must understand what they are agreeing to. Consent is not valid if the user does not know what the AI will do.

### Granular

Users should be able to allow or deny specific AI capabilities, not just everything at once.

### Revocable

Users must be able to withdraw consent at any time, with clear consequences.

### Contextual

Ask for permission at the moment it is needed, not at signup.

### Transparent

Explain how data will be used, how long it will be stored, and who can access it.

---

## 2. Consent Patterns

### Just-in-time consent

Ask for data or capability access when the AI needs it, not before.

Example: “Allow the AI to access your calendar to suggest meeting times?”

### Granular permissions

Break consent into separate, understandable permissions.

- Access files.
- Read emails.
- Send messages.
- Train on my data.

### Permission summaries

Show the user what the AI currently has access to and why.

- A single screen or panel with all active permissions.
- Easy toggles to turn each on or off.

### Data use disclosure

- What data is collected.
- Why it is collected.
- How long it is kept.
- Whether it is used to train models.
- How to delete or export it.

---

## 3. Control Patterns

### Capability toggles

Let users turn AI features on and off individually.

- “AI suggestions in this app.”
- “AI-generated summaries.”
- “AI chat assistant.”

### Data access controls

- Show which data sources the AI can access.
- Let users connect, disconnect, or limit access.
- Provide a clear view of what data is shared.

### Activity history

- Show what the AI has done.
- Let users review, delete, or correct past actions.

### Pause and disable

- Provide a global off switch.
- Let users pause the AI at any time.
- Make it easy to find the setting.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Use plain language, not legal or technical jargon.
- Ask for permission in context, not all at once.
- Provide granular options, not all-or-nothing.
- Make it easy to view and change permissions.
- Explain the benefit to the user.
- Be honest about the consequences of denying access.
- Do not make consent a condition of basic use unless necessary.
- Allow users to delete or export their data.
- Update users when AI capabilities or data use changes.

---

## 5. Common Mistakes

- Long, unreadable terms and conditions.
- Asking for all permissions at signup.
- No way to see or change permissions later.
- Making AI features all-or-nothing.
- Hiding how data is used to train models.
- Forcing consent to use the product.
- No activity history or logs.
- Difficult or hidden disable options.

---

## 6. Checklist

- [ ] Consent is asked for in context, not all at once.
- [ ] Users understand what they are agreeing to.
- [ ] Permissions are granular.
- [ ] Users can view all active permissions.
- [ ] Users can revoke or change permissions at any time.
- [ ] Data use, storage, and model training are disclosed.
- [ ] Activity history is available.
- [ ] Users can delete or export their data.
- [ ] AI can be paused or disabled easily.
- [ ] Permission language is plain and honest.
- [ ] Changes to AI capabilities or data use trigger updated consent.
