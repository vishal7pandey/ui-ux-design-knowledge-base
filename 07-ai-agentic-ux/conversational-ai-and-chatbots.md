# Conversational AI and Chatbots

Conversational interfaces let users interact through dialogue. Good chatbot UX feels natural, helpful, and efficient. Bad chatbot UX is frustrating and circular.

---

## 1. When to Use Conversational AI

Conversational AI is good for:

- Open-ended or exploratory tasks.
- Questions that are hard to express through forms.
- Quick answers and triage.
- Hands-free interaction.

Conversational AI is poor for:

- Tasks with many precise inputs.
- Visual or spatial tasks.
- Workflows where a GUI is faster.
- High-stakes decisions that need full context.

---

## 2. Chatbot UX Principles

### Start with a clear value

- The chatbot should say what it can do and how to begin.
- Offer example prompts.

### Keep turns focused

- One task or question per turn.
- Avoid long, multi-part messages.

### Show progress

- Display “typing,” “thinking,” or “working” indicators.
- Break long tasks into steps.

### Provide exits

- Make it easy to reset, end, or escalate to a human.
- Offer clear commands: “Start over,” “Talk to a person,” “Help.”

### Remember context

- Use short-term memory for the current conversation.
- Use long-term memory only with consent.

---

## 3. Chatbot Patterns

### Welcome message

- Greet the user.
- Explain what the bot can do.
- Provide 2–4 example prompts.

### Suggestion chips

- Quick-tap suggestions based on context.
- Reduce typing and guide users.

### Confirmation prompts

- Confirm before high-stakes actions.
- “I’ll book a table for 4 at 7 PM. Correct?”

### Disambiguation

- Ask clarifying questions when the user’s intent is unclear.
- Provide options, not open-ended guesses.

### Handoff

- Escalate to a human when the bot cannot help.
- Provide context and summary for the human.

### Fallback

- When the bot does not understand, respond helpfully.
- “I’m not sure I can help with that. Here are some things I can do: …”

---

## 4. Message Design

### Length

- Keep bot messages short.
- Use paragraphs, bullets, and numbered lists.
- For long answers, provide a summary first.

### Tone

- Match the brand and context.
- Be helpful, clear, and friendly.
- Avoid overly casual or robotic language.

### Formatting

- Use bold, lists, and cards to structure output.
- Show code, images, or links when relevant.

---

## 5. Accessibility

- Provide transcripts of spoken interactions.
- Make the chat keyboard navigable.
- Announce new messages to screen readers.
- Do not rely on color alone for meaning.
- Support text resizing and high contrast.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Overusing chat for tasks better done with forms.
- No clear welcome or examples.
- Long, unreadable bot messages.
- Circular conversations with no resolution.
- No way to talk to a human.
- Pretending the bot is human.
- Ignoring context and making users repeat themselves.
- No fallback for unknown questions.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Chatbot purpose and capabilities are clear from the start.
- [ ] Example prompts guide users.
- [ ] Suggestion chips reduce typing.
- [ ] Turns are short and focused.
- [ ] Progress is shown for long tasks.
- [ ] High-stakes actions are confirmed.
- [ ] Disambiguation is used for unclear input.
- [ ] Human handoff is available.
- [ ] Fallback responses are helpful.
- [ ] Output is well-formatted and scannable.
- [ ] Conversation context is maintained.
- [ ] Accessibility and keyboard navigation are supported.
- [ ] Users can reset, end, or get help.
