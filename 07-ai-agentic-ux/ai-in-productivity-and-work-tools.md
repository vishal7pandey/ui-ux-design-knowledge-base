# AI in Productivity and Work Tools

AI in productivity tools helps people manage tasks, time, communication, and content. The UX should reduce friction and help users focus on high-value work.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Reduce friction

- Automate repetitive tasks.
- Suggest next steps.
- Pre-fill and summarize where useful.

### Keep users in control

- Do not make decisions for users.
- Make AI actions easy to review and undo.
- Respect user preferences.

### Context-aware

- Use the current document, conversation, and calendar.
- Avoid asking for information the AI can already access.

### Focus

- Help users prioritize.
- Reduce noise and interruptions.
- Highlight what matters.

### Reliable

- Productivity AI must be trustworthy.
- Output should be accurate and useful.
- Let users verify important results.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Email drafting and triage.
- Meeting summaries and action items.
- Calendar scheduling.
- Task prioritization.
- Document drafting and editing.
- Note-taking and knowledge management.
- Translation and communication.
- Workflow automation.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Smart compose

- Suggest the next few words or sentences.
- User can accept or keep typing.
- Best for email, docs, and chat.

### Summarize

- Summarize long emails, documents, or meetings.
- Show key points and action items.
- Link to the original content.

### Action items

- Extract tasks from meetings or messages.
- Suggest owners and due dates.
- Let users confirm or edit.

### Schedule

- Suggest meeting times based on availability.
- Propose rescheduling.
- Let users approve.

### Smart search

- Find documents, messages, and files.
- Answer questions across the workspace.

### Inbox triage

- Sort, prioritize, and suggest replies.
- Let users review before sending.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Start with high-value, low-risk tasks.
- Make AI suggestions optional.
- Provide clear source and context.
- Let users approve high-stakes actions.
- Keep the UI clean and focused.
- Respect working hours and do-not-disturb.
- Support undo and version history.
- Be transparent about data access.

---

## 5. Privacy and Control

- Be clear about what data is accessed.
- Give users opt-out and data deletion.
- Do not use private data for training without consent.
- Respect boundaries between work and personal.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Over-automating sensitive tasks.
- Sending emails or scheduling without approval.
- Summaries that miss key points.
- Inaccurate action items.
- No way to undo or correct.
- Too many AI suggestions and interruptions.
- Not respecting user preferences.
- Using productivity data in unexpected ways.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] AI automates repetitive, low-stakes work.
- [ ] High-stakes actions require approval.
- [ ] Suggestions are optional and easy to reject.
- [ ] Summaries capture key points and action items.
- [ ] Scheduling and communication respect user preferences.
- [ ] Search answers are sourced and verifiable.
- [ ] Inbox triage is accurate and controllable.
- [ ] Undo and version history are available.
- [ ] Data access and privacy are transparent.
- [ ] The tool helps users focus, not adds noise.
- [ ] Productivity gains are measured.
- [ ] Users trust and feel in control of the AI.
