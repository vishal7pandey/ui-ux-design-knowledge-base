# AI in Food and Nutrition UX

AI in food and nutrition helps users plan meals, understand ingredients, track intake, and make healthier choices. The UX must be accurate, inclusive, and health-conscious.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Accuracy

- Nutrition data must be correct.
- Recommendations should be evidence-based.
- Avoid unverified health claims.

### Personalization

- Adapt to dietary needs, allergies, culture, and goals.
- Respect medical conditions and preferences.

### Inclusivity

- Support different cuisines, budgets, and lifestyles.
- Avoid shaming or moralizing food choices.

### Health and safety

- Consider allergies, intolerances, and medical conditions.
- Recommend professional advice when needed.

### Privacy

- Food and health data is sensitive.
- Protect it and give users control.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Meal planning.
- Recipe recommendations.
- Nutrition tracking and analysis.
- Food logging from photos or text.
- Dietary restriction management.
- Grocery shopping and substitutions.
- Allergen detection.
- Cooking guidance.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Meal planner

- Generate weekly or daily meal plans.
- Consider dietary goals and restrictions.
- Let users swap and adjust.

### Recipe recommendations

- Suggest recipes based on ingredients, time, and skill.
- Show nutrition and serving size.
- Filter by diet and allergens.

### Food logging

- Log by photo, barcode, or text.
- Show estimated nutrition.
- Let users correct errors.

### Nutrition insights

- Track macros, calories, and micronutrients.
- Show trends and progress.
- Provide context, not judgment.

### Substitutions

- Suggest alternatives for missing ingredients.
- Show dietary-friendly swaps.

### Allergen and preference checks

- Highlight allergens in recipes.
- Warn about cross-contamination if applicable.
- Respect vegan, halal, kosher, and other preferences.

### Cooking coach

- Guide users step by step.
- Adjust for skill and equipment.
- Answer questions during cooking.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Use accurate, up-to-date nutrition databases.
- Disclose when nutrition is estimated.
- Support a wide range of diets and allergies.
- Avoid diet culture or shaming language.
- Provide balanced, sustainable guidance.
- Encourage professional advice for medical conditions.
- Make logging fast and easy.
- Show portion sizes clearly.
- Test with diverse users and cuisines.
- Protect food and health data.

---

## 5. Disclaimers

- “This is not medical or dietetic advice.”
- “Consult a healthcare professional for medical conditions.”
- “Nutrition information is estimated and may vary.”

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Inaccurate nutrition data.
- Not accounting for allergies or restrictions.
- Promoting extreme or unbalanced diets.
- Shaming users for food choices.
- No portion size context.
- Over-reliance on photo logging.
- Ignoring cultural food differences.
- Collecting health data without consent.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Nutrition data is accurate and sourced.
- [ ] Meal plans respect dietary needs and goals.
- [ ] Recipes are filterable by allergens and preferences.
- [ ] Food logging is fast and easy to correct.
- [ ] Insights are contextual and non-judgmental.
- [ ] Substitutions and swaps are provided.
- [ ] Allergen warnings are clear.
- [ ] Cooking guidance adapts to skill and equipment.
- [ ] Disclaimers are visible.
- [ ] Privacy and data use are transparent.
- [ ] Guidance is evidence-based and balanced.
- [ ] The product is tested with diverse users.
