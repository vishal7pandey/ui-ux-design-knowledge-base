# AI in Logistics and Supply Chain UX

AI in logistics and supply chain helps move goods efficiently. The UX must support planners, drivers, warehouse workers, and customers with accurate, timely, and actionable information.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Visibility

- Show where goods are, where they are going, and when they will arrive.
- Provide real-time or near real-time tracking.

### Predictability

- Use AI to forecast delays, demand, and inventory needs.
- Set clear expectations for users.

### Efficiency

- Optimize routes, loads, and schedules.
- Reduce waste and cost.

### Resilience

- Flag disruptions and suggest alternatives.
- Support rapid replanning.

### Collaboration

- Share information across teams, partners, and customers.
- Keep everyone aligned.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Route optimization.
- Demand forecasting.
- Inventory management.
- Warehouse picking and packing.
- Shipment tracking.
- Supplier risk assessment.
- Last-mile delivery.
- Returns and reverse logistics.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Tracking map

- Show current location and expected route.
- Display ETAs and milestones.
- Update in real time.

### Demand forecast

- Show predicted demand over time.
- Highlight confidence intervals.
- Let planners adjust assumptions.

### Inventory dashboard

- Show stock levels, reorder points, and risks.
- Recommend actions.
- Link to suppliers and purchase orders.

### Route planner

- Optimize routes based on constraints.
- Show alternative routes.
- Let dispatchers approve or edit.

### Disruption alerts

- Notify about delays, weather, or supplier issues.
- Suggest alternative actions.
- Estimate impact.

### Warehouse guidance

- Direct workers to pick, pack, and stow locations.
- Use clear visuals and voice where needed.
- Prioritize tasks.

### Customer notifications

- Proactive updates about delivery status.
- Easy rescheduling or address changes.
- Transparent ETAs.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Show real-time data where possible.
- Provide ETA with confidence.
- Make delays and issues clear early.
- Allow users to drill into the data.
- Support mobile and rugged devices.
- Integrate with WMS, TMS, and ERP systems.
- Use plain language for alerts.
- Let planners override AI recommendations.
- Protect shipment and partner data.
- Test in real logistics environments.

---

## 5. Privacy and Trust

- Protect customer addresses and purchase data.
- Be clear about tracking and data use.
- Avoid using driver data punitively.
- Secure partner and supplier information.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Inaccurate ETAs and forecasts.
- Over-complex dashboards.
- No early warning about delays.
- No way for users to override plans.
- Not supporting mobile devices.
- Ignoring last-mile complexity.
- Using data to penalize workers.
- Not integrating with existing systems.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Tracking shows real-time location and ETA.
- [ ] Forecasts include confidence and assumptions.
- [ ] Inventory levels and risks are visible.
- [ ] Route and load optimization are explainable.
- [ ] Disruptions are communicated with alternatives.
- [ ] Warehouse guidance is clear and task-oriented.
- [ ] Customer updates are proactive and transparent.
- [ ] Planners can override AI recommendations.
- [ ] Mobile and rugged devices are supported.
- [ ] Integrations with WMS, TMS, and ERP exist.
- [ ] Data privacy and partner trust are protected.
- [ ] The product is tested in real logistics settings.
