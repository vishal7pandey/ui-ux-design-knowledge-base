# AI in Science and Research UX

AI in science and research accelerates discovery, literature review, and data analysis. The UX must prioritize accuracy, reproducibility, and scientific rigor.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Accuracy

- Scientific claims must be correct.
- AI should not invent data or citations.
- Outputs must be verifiable.

### Reproducibility

- Show methods, data, and parameters.
- Allow others to reproduce results.
- Track versions and changes.

### Transparency

- Explain how AI contributed to a finding.
- Disclose limitations and confidence.
- Cite sources.

### Human oversight

- Researchers make scientific judgments.
- AI supports, does not replace, the scientific method.

### Collaboration

- Support teams and peer review.
- Share results and methods securely.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Literature review and synthesis.
- Hypothesis generation.
- Data cleaning and analysis.
- Experiment design.
- Protein and drug discovery.
- Climate and environmental modeling.
- Simulation and prediction.
- Writing and formatting papers.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Literature search

- Search across papers with natural language.
- Summarize key findings.
- Link to original sources.

### Research assistant

- Answer questions about methods, papers, and data.
- Provide citations.
- Suggest related work.

### Data analysis

- Clean, analyze, and visualize data.
- Show code, assumptions, and statistical tests.
- Allow researchers to review and modify.

### Experiment planner

- Suggest variables, controls, and sample sizes.
- Explain the design rationale.
- Help with power analysis.

### Hypothesis explorer

- Identify patterns and propose hypotheses.
- Clearly mark hypotheses as speculative.
- Link to supporting data.

### Simulation and modeling

- Run and compare models.
- Show inputs, outputs, and parameters.
- Allow parameter tuning.

### Paper writing

- Draft sections with citations.
- Format for journals.
- Ensure attribution and originality.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Always cite sources and provide data links.
- Show confidence and uncertainty.
- Make methods and parameters visible.
- Support version control and provenance.
- Allow researchers to edit and verify.
- Integrate with reference managers and data repositories.
- Protect research data and intellectual property.
- Encourage peer review and human validation.
- Avoid overclaiming what the AI can do.

---

## 5. Reproducibility and Provenance

- Track all data, code, and model versions.
- Record every AI-assisted step.
- Provide notebooks or reports that can be re-run.
- Link to public or shared datasets where possible.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Fabricated or hallucinated citations.
- Over-confident predictions without validation.
- Hiding the AI role in research.
- Not sharing methods or data.
- Biased or unrepresentative training data.
- Replacing peer review with AI.
- Inaccurate summarization of papers.
- Not checking generated code or analysis.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Literature searches are sourced and linked.
- [ ] Summaries preserve the original meaning.
- [ ] Hypotheses are clearly marked and supported.
- [ ] Data analysis is reproducible and documented.
- [ ] Methods, parameters, and code are visible.
- [ ] Confidence and uncertainty are shown.
- [ ] AI contributions are disclosed.
- [ ] Citations are verified and accurate.
- [ ] Research data is protected.
- [ ] Version control and provenance are maintained.
- [ ] Peer review and human validation are encouraged.
- [ ] The product supports scientific rigor and integrity.
