# AI in Utilities and Energy UX

AI in utilities and energy helps manage grids, reduce consumption, predict demand, and maintain infrastructure. The UX must be reliable, clear, and actionable for operators, consumers, and field workers.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Reliability

- Grid and energy decisions are critical.
- Data and predictions must be accurate.
- Downtime can be costly or dangerous.

### Real-time

- Energy systems operate continuously.
- Updates, alerts, and controls must be timely.

### Efficiency

- Help reduce waste and cost.
- Optimize generation, distribution, and use.

### Safety

- Protect workers and the public.
- Provide clear warnings and lockouts.

### Sustainability

- Support renewable integration.
- Help users understand and reduce their carbon footprint.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Demand forecasting.
- Grid management and optimization.
- Predictive maintenance.
- Energy usage monitoring.
- Outage detection and response.
- Renewable integration.
- Smart home energy management.
- Billing and rate optimization.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Grid operations dashboard

- Show generation, load, and balance.
- Highlight anomalies and constraints.
- Provide control for operators.

### Demand forecast

- Show predicted vs. actual demand.
- Use confidence intervals.
- Let operators adjust assumptions.

### Predictive maintenance

- Alert before equipment fails.
- Show risk, recommended action, and timing.
- Schedule work.

### Consumer energy portal

- Show usage, cost, and trends.
- Provide tips to save.
- Compare to similar homes.
- Set goals and alerts.

### Outage map

- Show current and predicted outages.
- Provide ETA for restoration.
- Allow users to report issues.

### Smart home energy

- Optimize heating, cooling, and EV charging.
- Schedule based on rates and renewable availability.
- Let users approve changes.

### Billing insights

- Show bill breakdown and rate plan.
- Recommend better plans.
- Explain charges.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Make critical information glanceable.
- Use clear, color-coded alerts.
- Provide real-time and historical views.
- Support mobile for field workers.
- Integrate with SCADA, AMI, and other systems.
- Allow operators to override AI.
- Protect grid and customer data.
- Explain energy-saving tips simply.
- Test in real operations centers.
- Support demand response programs.

---

## 5. Safety and Privacy

- Protect critical infrastructure.
- Restrict access to controls.
- Secure customer usage data.
- Be clear about data collection.
- Provide opt-out for non-essential monitoring.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Slow or stale data.
- Overloaded dashboards.
- Unclear alerts.
- No way to override AI decisions.
- Not integrating with existing systems.
- Complex pricing that users do not understand.
- Weak security on critical systems.
- Not supporting field use.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Grid and demand data is real-time and accurate.
- [ ] Critical alerts are clear and actionable.
- [ ] Operators can override AI recommendations.
- [ ] Forecasts include confidence and assumptions.
- [ ] Predictive maintenance is timely and reliable.
- [ ] Consumer portals are clear and motivating.
- [ ] Outage maps are accurate and updated.
- [ ] Smart home energy changes are user-approved.
- [ ] Billing is transparent and understandable.
- [ ] Field workers have mobile access.
- [ ] Security and privacy are strong.
- [ ] Systems are tested in real operations.
