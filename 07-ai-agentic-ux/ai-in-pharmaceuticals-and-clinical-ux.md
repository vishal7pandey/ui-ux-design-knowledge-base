# AI in Pharmaceuticals and Clinical UX

AI in pharmaceuticals and clinical settings supports drug discovery, trial management, and clinical decision support. The UX must be precise, compliant, and protective of patient safety.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Safety

- Patient safety is the top priority.
- AI must not replace clinical judgment.
- Provide clear disclaimers and escalation.

### Accuracy

- Clinical data must be correct.
- Predictions and recommendations must be validated.
- Cite evidence and guidelines.

### Compliance

- Follow FDA, EMA, HIPAA, GCP, and other regulations.
- Maintain audit trails.
- Document model validation.

### Explainability

- Clinicians need to understand recommendations.
- Show reasoning, evidence, and confidence.

### Privacy

- Patient data is highly sensitive.
- Strong security and consent are required.

### Human oversight

- Clinicians make final decisions.
- AI supports diagnosis, treatment, and research.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Drug discovery and molecular design.
- Clinical trial matching and recruitment.
- Trial monitoring and adverse event detection.
- Clinical decision support.
- Drug interaction checking.
- Dosing recommendations.
- Diagnostics and imaging.
- Regulatory documentation.
- Pharmacovigilance.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Drug discovery

- Visualize molecular structures and properties.
- Show predictions and confidence.
- Let researchers compare compounds.

### Trial matching

- Match patients to eligible trials.
- Show inclusion/exclusion criteria.
- Explain the match.

### Clinical decision support

- Show relevant evidence at the point of care.
- Highlight drug interactions and contraindications.
- Provide confidence and references.

### Adverse event detection

- Monitor and flag potential adverse events.
- Show supporting data.
- Allow clinicians to review and report.

### Imaging and diagnostics

- Highlight regions of interest.
- Show confidence and differential diagnosis.
- Provide original images for review.

### Dosing assistant

- Recommend dosing based on patient factors.
- Show calculations and references.
- Require clinician confirmation.

### Regulatory and documentation

- Draft sections of regulatory submissions.
- Maintain version control and audit trails.
- Link to source data.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Validate all AI outputs before clinical use.
- Provide clear, evidence-based recommendations.
- Cite sources and guidelines.
- Keep clinicians in control.
- Maintain strict data security and privacy.
- Follow regulatory requirements.
- Audit for bias and fairness.
- Support adverse event reporting.
- Provide clear disclaimers.
- Test with clinicians and researchers.
- Ensure interoperability with EHR and other systems.

---

## 5. Disclaimers

- “This tool is for decision support, not diagnosis or treatment.”
- “Always verify with current clinical guidelines and patient context.”
- “AI recommendations must be reviewed by a qualified clinician.”

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Providing diagnosis or treatment advice.
- Outdated or inaccurate clinical data.
- No source citations.
- Overconfidence in predictions.
- Weak data security.
- No audit trail.
- Replacing clinician judgment.
- Bias in training data.
- Not reporting adverse events.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Outputs are validated and evidence-based.
- [ ] Sources and clinical guidelines are cited.
- [ ] Confidence and uncertainty are shown.
- [ ] Disclaimers are clear and prominent.
- [ ] Clinician review and confirmation are required.
- [ ] Patient data is protected.
- [ ] Regulatory and compliance requirements are met.
- [ ] Audit trails are maintained.
- [ ] Bias and fairness are tested.
- [ ] Adverse events can be reported and tracked.
- [ ] EHR and clinical systems are integrated.
- [ ] The product is tested with clinicians and researchers.
