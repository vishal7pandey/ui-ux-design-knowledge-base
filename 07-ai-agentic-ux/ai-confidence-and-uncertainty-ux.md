# AI Confidence and Uncertainty UX

AI systems are not always right. Showing confidence and uncertainty helps users decide how much to trust an answer and what to do next.

---

## 1. Why Confidence and Uncertainty Matter

- Users need to know what to trust.
- Overconfident AI leads to bad decisions.
- Honest uncertainty builds trust over time.
- It helps users know when to verify or ask again.

---

## 2. Levels of Confidence

### High confidence

The AI is confident based on strong evidence or clear sources.

- Presentation: direct, brief.
- Example: “This is a direct match to your document.”

### Medium confidence

The AI has some evidence but there is room for doubt.

- Presentation: qualify the answer.
- Example: “This is likely, but I recommend checking the source.”

### Low confidence

The AI is unsure or guessing.

- Presentation: hedge, show alternatives, or ask for clarification.
- Example: “I’m not sure. Here are a few possibilities: …”

---

## 3. How to Show Confidence

### Confidence score

- A percentage, color, or label.
- Use sparingly; not all users understand percentages.
- Pair with plain language.

### Source count

- “Based on 5 documents.”
- “Found in 1 source.”
- More sources generally means more reliable.

### Evidence links

- Link to the source material.
- Let users verify themselves.

### Qualifying language

- “It appears that…”
- “One likely explanation is…”
- “I could not find a definitive answer.”

### Alternative answers

- Show a ranked list of possibilities.
- Let the user pick the most relevant.

---

## 4. Uncertainty Patterns

### “I don’t know”

- Be direct: “I don’t have enough information to answer that.”
- Do not guess when stakes are high.

### Partial answer

- “I found some related information, but not a complete answer.”
- Show what you have and what is missing.

### Ask for clarification

- “Do you mean X or Y?”
- “Can you provide more context?”

### Disclaimers

- “AI-generated content. Please verify before using.”
- Use for high-stakes or factual domains.

---

## 5. When to Show Uncertainty

- When the AI is unsure.
- When there are conflicting sources.
- When the question is outside the AI’s knowledge.
- When the answer is predictive or speculative.
- When the user asks for facts that can be verified.

---

## 6. Best Practices

- Be honest, not apologetic.
- Use plain language.
- Show why the AI is confident or uncertain.
- Provide a way to verify.
- Offer next steps: rephrase, add context, check source.
- Do not fake confidence to please the user.
- Match the level of warning to the risk.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

- Always sounding certain.
- Hiding uncertainty behind vague language.
- No way to see sources.
- Overusing percentage scores without explanation.
- Showing low confidence in every answer.
- Not adjusting tone for high-stakes answers.
- Pretending to know when the AI does not.

---

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Confidence is communicated in plain language.
- [ ] High, medium, and low confidence are distinguishable.
- [ ] Sources and evidence are linked.
- [ ] Uncertainty is shown for speculative or weak answers.
- [ ] Users know when to verify or ask for clarification.
- [ ] “I don’t know” is an acceptable output.
- [ ] Disclaimers appear for high-stakes or factual content.
- [ ] Confidence visuals are consistent.
- [ ] Alternatives or next steps are offered.
- [ ] The AI does not pretend to know what it does not.
