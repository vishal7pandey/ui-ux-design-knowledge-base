# AI Recommendations and Predictive UX

AI can recommend next steps, content, and actions. Predictive UX anticipates what the user wants. Done well, it saves time. Done poorly, it feels intrusive or wrong.

---

## 1. Types of AI Recommendations

| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| **Content** | “Next article to read” or “Video you might like.” |
| **Action** | “Reply with thanks” or “Schedule this.” |
| **Product** | “Customers also bought” or “Upgrade to this plan.” |
| **People** | “Suggested contacts” or “Expert to follow.” |
| **Workflow** | “Create a project from this email.” |

---

## 2. Recommendation Principles

### Relevance

- Recommendations should be based on real user needs.
- Avoid generic, one-size-fits-all suggestions.
- Use context, history, and intent.

### Timeliness

- Suggest at the right moment.
- Avoid interrupting the current task.
- Use quiet, contextual signals.

### Transparency

- Explain why something is recommended.
- “Based on your recent projects.”
- Let users see and manage the data used.

### Control

- Let users accept, reject, or ignore.
- Provide a way to turn recommendations off.
- Let users improve recommendations through feedback.

---

## 3. Predictive UX Patterns

### Suggestion chips

- Small, tappable suggestions below a search or input.
- Low friction and easy to ignore.

### Next best action

- A single, highlighted action the user might want.
- “You have 3 unread emails. Reply to the most urgent?”

### Pre-fill

- Predict what the user wants and fill it in.
- Let them edit or accept.

### Smart defaults

- Pre-select the most likely option.
- Reduce decision-making.

### Proactive alerts

- Notify when the AI detects something important.
- Keep alerts rare and high-value.

---

## 4. Feedback and Learning

- Thumbs up/down on recommendations.
- “Not interested” or “See fewer like this.”
- “Why am I seeing this?”
- Use feedback to improve future recommendations.

---

## 5. Privacy and Trust

- Be transparent about what data is used.
- Do not creep users out with overly specific predictions.
- Let users opt out.
- Avoid using sensitive data without consent.
- Honor data retention and deletion requests.

---

## 6. Best Practices

- Start with high-confidence, useful recommendations.
- Keep recommendations relevant and non-intrusive.
- Explain the “why” behind suggestions.
- Let users easily accept, reject, or ignore.
- Use feedback to improve.
- Test with real users.
- Balance personalization with privacy.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

- Recommending irrelevant or outdated content.
- Too many recommendations at once.
- Not explaining why something is recommended.
- Interrupting users with low-value suggestions.
- Creepy or overly personal predictions.
- No way to turn off or adjust recommendations.
- Using recommendations to manipulate behavior.
- Not learning from user feedback.

---

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Recommendations are relevant to the user’s context.
- [ ] Suggestions are shown at the right time.
- [ ] Users understand why something is recommended.
- [ ] Users can accept, reject, or ignore.
- [ ] Recommendations do not interrupt core tasks.
- [ ] Feedback on recommendations is collected.
- [ ] Privacy and data use are transparent.
- [ ] Users can disable or adjust recommendations.
- [ ] Predictions are tested for accuracy and usefulness.
- [ ] Recommendations improve over time based on feedback.
