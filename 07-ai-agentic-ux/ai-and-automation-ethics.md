# AI and Automation Ethics

Automation can free people from repetitive work, but it can also displace jobs, reduce agency, and create unfair outcomes. Ethical automation design considers the human impact.

---

## 1. Core Ethical Questions

- Who benefits from the automation?
- Who is harmed or displaced?
- Who is accountable for the outcome?
- Who has the power to change or stop it?
- Is the automation transparent and explainable?

---

## 2. Principles of Ethical Automation

### Human agency

People should retain meaningful control. Automation should support, not replace, human judgment where it matters.

### Transparency

Users should understand what is automated, why, and how it works.

### Accountability

There must be a clear human or organizational owner for automated decisions.

### Fairness

Automation should not disproportionately harm vulnerable or underrepresented groups.

### Safety

Automation should not put people, data, or systems at risk.

### Dignity

Work should not be reduced to surveillance or dehumanization.

---

## 3. Designing Ethical Automation

### Preserve human judgment

- Keep humans in the loop for high-stakes decisions.
- Provide override and appeal.
- Allow users to question or reject automated actions.

### Avoid surveillance automation

- Do not monitor employees or users in ways that erode trust.
- Be clear about what data is collected and why.
- Use data for support, not punishment.

### Explain automation

- Tell users when an action is automated.
- Show the logic and data behind the decision.
- Provide recourse.

### Measure impact

- Track who is affected by automation.
- Look for unintended consequences.
- Adjust when harm is found.

### Include affected people

- Involve workers, users, and communities in design.
- Consider how automation changes jobs and lives.

---

## 4. Common Ethical Risks

- Job displacement without reskilling.
- Algorithmic discrimination.
- Loss of human oversight.
- Opacity in automated decisions.
- Over-monitoring of workers.
- Concentration of power.
- Environmental costs of training large models.

---

## 5. Mitigations

- Provide reskilling and transition support.
- Test for disparate impact.
- Implement human-in-the-loop for decisions that affect people.
- Publish clear policies and explanations.
- Use data responsibly and with consent.
- Design for sustainability.

---

## 6. Checklist

- [ ] The purpose and impact of automation are clear.
- [ ] Humans retain control over high-stakes decisions.
- [ ] Users know when an action is automated.
- [ ] The logic behind automation is explainable.
- [ ] Accountability is assigned.
- [ ] Fairness and disparate impact are tested.
- [ ] Surveillance is avoided.
- [ ] Affected people are included in design.
- [ ] Recourse and appeal are available.
- [ ] Environmental and social costs are considered.
- [ ] Automation is monitored and adjusted over time.
