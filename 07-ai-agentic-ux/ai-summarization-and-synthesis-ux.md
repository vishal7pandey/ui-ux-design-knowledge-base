# AI Summarization and Synthesis UX

Summarization and synthesis help users make sense of large amounts of information. AI can condense, connect, and present content in a useful form.

---

## 1. What Is Summarization?

Summarization reduces content to its most important points.

### Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Extractive** | Pulls key sentences from the source. |
| **Abstractive** | Rewrites the content in new words. |
| **Bullet summary** | Lists key points. |
| **Narrative summary** | Short paragraph. |
| **Headline** | One-line version. |

---

## 2. What Is Synthesis?

Synthesis combines information from multiple sources to create new understanding.

- Identify themes, patterns, and conflicts.
- Connect ideas across sources.
- Present a coherent overview.

---

## 3. UX Principles

### Faithful to source

- Summary should not add or distort meaning.
- Synthesis should represent the sources fairly.

### Scannable

- Use bullets, headings, and short paragraphs.
- Let users quickly see the key points.

### Verifiable

- Link to the original content.
- Let users see what the summary was based on.

### Adjustable

- Let users choose length and format.
- “Make shorter,” “make longer,” “more detailed.”

### Contextual

- Tailor the summary to the user’s goal.
- “Summary for a busy executive” vs. “summary for a new team member.”

---

## 4. Patterns

### Document summary

- Summarize a single document.
- Show key points, topics, and action items.
- Link to sections.

### Thread or conversation summary

- Summarize a long chat or email thread.
- Show the main points and decisions.

### Multi-source synthesis

- Combine several sources into one overview.
- Show themes, conflicts, and gaps.

### Comparative summary

- Compare two or more documents side by side.
- Highlight similarities and differences.

### Update summary

- Show what changed since the last version.
- Useful for ongoing projects or reports.

---

## 5. Output Formats

- Bullet list.
- Numbered steps.
- Table.
- Key quote highlights.
- Timeline.
- Mind map or outline.
- Narrative paragraph.

---

## 6. Best Practices

- Preserve nuance and avoid oversimplification.
- Always link to the original content.
- Let users adjust the length and style.
- Show what is being summarized.
- Provide confidence or coverage notes.
- Avoid copying without attribution.
- Use synthesis to connect, not to invent.
- Test summaries with real readers.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

- Losing important details or nuance.
- Hallucinating content not in the source.
- No source links.
- Overly generic summaries.
- Synthesis that introduces bias.
- Not letting users adjust the output.
- Copying without attribution.
- Long, un-scannable blocks of text.

---

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] Summaries are faithful to the source.
- [ ] Source links are provided.
- [ ] Users can choose length and format.
- [ ] Output is scannable.
- [ ] Synthesis connects sources without inventing content.
- [ ] Key themes and conflicts are highlighted.
- [ ] Confidence and coverage are noted.
- [ ] Summaries are tested for accuracy.
- [ ] Users can verify and expand points.
- [ ] Format matches the user’s task.
