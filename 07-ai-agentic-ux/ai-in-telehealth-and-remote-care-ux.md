# AI in Telehealth and Remote Care UX

AI in telehealth and remote care helps patients and providers connect, triage, and manage care outside of traditional facilities. The UX must be safe, accessible, and trustworthy.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Safety

- Remote care is not a replacement for emergency services.
- Triage must be accurate and cautious.
- Always provide clear escalation.

### Trust

- Patients and providers must trust the technology.
- Be transparent about AI use and limits.
- Protect privacy and data.

### Accessibility

- Serve patients with different devices, languages, and abilities.
- Support low bandwidth and older devices.

### Human connection

- AI should support, not replace, the patient-provider relationship.
- Make it easy to reach a human.

### Equity

- Do not exclude underserved populations.
- Provide interpretation and local-language support.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Symptom triage.
- Pre-visit intake.
- Remote monitoring.
- Medication adherence.
- Chronic disease management.
- Mental health support.
- Provider documentation.
- Follow-up and care plans.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Pre-visit intake

- Collect symptoms, history, and concerns.
- Use plain language and simple questions.
- Summarize for the provider.

### Symptom triage

- Ask a short series of questions.
- Recommend care level: self-care, telehealth, in-person, emergency.
- Explain the recommendation.

### Remote monitoring

- Track vitals, symptoms, and activity.
- Show trends and alerts.
- Notify providers of concerning changes.

### Medication reminders

- Send reminders and track adherence.
- Allow patients to log doses.
- Alert providers to missed doses.

### Chronic care support

- Provide education and check-ins.
- Track goals and progress.
- Adjust recommendations with provider input.

### Provider documentation

- Transcribe and summarize visits.
- Draft notes for provider review.
- Maintain accuracy and privacy.

### Mental health check-in

- Screen for distress or crisis.
- Provide resources and escalation.
- Use empathetic, non-judgmental language.

### Video and chat visits

- Provide clear technical setup.
- Show provider and patient instructions.
- Integrate AI notes and summaries.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Use clear, empathetic language.
- Avoid alarming or minimizing serious symptoms.
- Provide emergency guidance when needed.
- Protect patient data end-to-end.
- Support multiple languages and accessibility.
- Make human escalation easy.
- Integrate with EHR and provider workflows.
- Allow patients to review and correct information.
- Test with diverse patient populations.
- Follow healthcare regulations and standards.

---

## 5. Safety and Escalation

- “If you are experiencing a medical emergency, call your local emergency number.”
- “This is not a diagnosis. A provider should review your symptoms.”
- “Your results look unusual. We are connecting you with a nurse.”

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Under-triaging serious symptoms.
- Over-triaging minor issues and overwhelming providers.
- No clear emergency path.
- Impersonal or cold language.
- Not supporting low-tech patients.
- Data privacy issues.
- Replacing provider judgment.
- Inaccurate or biased symptom checkers.
- No way to contact a human.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Intake and triage are clear and cautious.
- [ ] Emergency escalation is prominent.
- [ ] Recommendations are explained.
- [ ] Remote monitoring is easy and actionable.
- [ ] Patients can review and correct data.
- [ ] Provider documentation is accurate and reviewed.
- [ ] Mental health and crisis resources are available.
- [ ] Video and chat visits are accessible.
- [ ] Multi-language and accessibility support is provided.
- [ ] Privacy and security meet healthcare standards.
- [ ] Human providers are easy to reach.
- [ ] The product is tested with diverse patients and providers.
