# AI in B2B and Enterprise UX

B2B and enterprise AI must be reliable, secure, explainable, and controllable. Buyers and users have high expectations for accuracy, compliance, and ROI.

---

## 1. Key Differences from Consumer AI

| Dimension | B2B/Enterprise | Consumer |
|-----------|----------------|----------|
| **Decision stakes** | Higher. | Lower. |
| **Users** | Multiple roles and permissions. | Individual. |
| **Data** | Sensitive, proprietary, regulated. | Personal. |
| **Trust** | Requires proof and compliance. | Built through experience. |
| **Integration** | Must connect to existing tools. | Standalone. |
| **ROI** | Must show measurable value. | Delight and engagement. |

---

## 2. Enterprise AI Principles

### Reliability

- Output must be accurate and consistent.
- Downtime is costly.
- SLAs and support matter.

### Security and privacy

- Data must be protected.
- Compliance with SOC 2, GDPR, HIPAA, and others.
- Clear data handling and retention policies.

### Explainability

- Users need to understand and defend AI decisions.
- Auditors and regulators may require explanations.
- Source attribution and reasoning are essential.

### Control

- Admins set policies.
- Users can override or adjust.
- Role-based access and permissions.

### Integration

- AI should work with existing tools and data.
- APIs, connectors, and workflows are important.

---

## 3. Common B2B AI Use Cases

- Document analysis and summarization.
- Customer support and ticket triage.
- Code review and documentation.
- Sales forecasting and lead scoring.
- Workflow automation.
- Compliance and risk monitoring.
- Data analysis and reporting.

---

## 4. UX Patterns

### Admin controls

- Central settings for AI features.
- Data and permission management.
- Model selection and limits.

### Audit logs

- Track what the AI did and who approved it.
- Searchable, exportable records.

### Confidence and evidence

- Show sources and reasoning.
- Highlight uncertain or flagged outputs.

### Human approval

- Require approval for high-stakes actions.
- Escalation queues and review.

### Workflow integration

- Embed AI into existing tools.
- Pass data between systems.
- Trigger actions in other apps.

### Role-based UX

- Different features for different roles.
- Admin, operator, reviewer, viewer.

---

## 5. Best Practices

- Start with high-value, low-risk use cases.
- Show ROI and time savings.
- Make the AI secure by default.
- Provide strong admin controls.
- Build for audit and compliance.
- Integrate with enterprise tools.
- Support SSO, SCIM, and role-based access.
- Train and onboard users for enterprise workflows.
- Provide enterprise support and SLAs.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Selling AI as a magic solution.
- Ignoring security and compliance.
- No admin or governance controls.
- Not integrating with existing tools.
- Over-promising accuracy.
- No audit trail.
- No human approval for high-stakes actions.
- Treating enterprise users like consumers.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] AI use cases are tied to business value.
- [ ] Security, privacy, and compliance are addressed.
- [ ] Admin controls and role-based access exist.
- [ ] Audit logs are available.
- [ ] Output is explainable and sourced.
- [ ] High-stakes actions require approval.
- [ ] Integrations with enterprise tools are provided.
- [ ] ROI and time savings are visible.
- [ ] Users are trained and onboarded.
- [ ] Enterprise support and SLAs are in place.
- [ ] The product is reliable and performant.
