# AI in Warehousing and Inventory UX

AI in warehousing and inventory helps manage stock, optimize storage, and speed fulfillment. The UX must support warehouse workers, managers, and systems with clear, real-time information.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Accuracy

- Inventory data must be correct.
- Mistakes lead to stockouts, overstock, and lost orders.

### Speed

- Warehouses operate at high velocity.
- UX must support fast, efficient work.

### Visibility

- Show what is where, how much, and when it moves.
- Real-time updates are critical.

### Efficiency

- Optimize picking, packing, and storage.
- Reduce walking, errors, and waste.

### Worker-friendly

- Interfaces should be simple and hands-free where possible.
- Support voice, scan, and wearable devices.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Inventory tracking and counts.
- Demand forecasting and replenishment.
- Pick path optimization.
- Slotting and storage optimization.
- Receiving and put-away.
- Cycle counting.
- Order packing and verification.
- Warehouse layout and design.
- Returns processing.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Inventory dashboard

- Show stock levels, locations, and movement.
- Highlight low stock, overstock, and discrepancies.
- Drill down to lot, expiry, and serial data.

### Pick and pack guide

- Direct workers through optimal pick paths.
- Show item, quantity, and location.
- Confirm each pick with scan or voice.

### Receiving and put-away

- Suggest where to put incoming goods.
- Update inventory in real time.
- Flag damaged or incorrect items.

### Cycle count

- Assign counts based on risk and history.
- Guide workers through the process.
- Reconcile discrepancies.

### Replenishment alerts

- Alert when stock is low.
- Recommend reorder quantities and timing.
- Let managers approve.

### Storage slotting

- Recommend where to store items.
- Optimize for velocity, size, and temperature.

### Returns processing

- Identify returned items.
- Suggest disposition: restock, refurbish, dispose.
- Update inventory.

### Warehouse map

- Show layout, zones, and current tasks.
- Help workers navigate.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Use barcodes, RFID, and scanners heavily.
- Support voice and wearable devices.
- Provide real-time inventory visibility.
- Optimize pick paths to reduce walking.
- Show clear task lists and priorities.
- Integrate with WMS, ERP, and order systems.
- Support batch, zone, and wave picking.
- Track lot, expiry, and serial numbers.
- Provide easy error correction.
- Train workers on AI tools.
- Test in real warehouse environments.

---

## 5. Common Mistakes

- Inaccurate or stale inventory data.
- Over-complex picking instructions.
- Not supporting hands-free operation.
- Poor integration with scanning.
- No clear error handling.
- Pick paths that are inefficient.
- Missing lot and expiry tracking.
- Not accounting for returns and damages.
- Ignoring worker feedback.

---

## 6. Checklist

- [ ] Inventory levels are accurate and real-time.
- [ ] Pick and pack instructions are clear and fast.
- [ ] Put-away and receiving are efficient.
- [ ] Cycle counts are guided and reconciled.
- [ ] Replenishment alerts are actionable.
- [ ] Storage slotting optimizes for velocity and constraints.
- [ ] Returns are processed and tracked.
- [ ] Warehouse maps are clear and updated.
- [ ] Scanning, voice, and wearables are supported.
- [ ] WMS, ERP, and order systems are integrated.
- [ ] Errors are easy to correct.
- [ ] The product is tested with warehouse workers.
