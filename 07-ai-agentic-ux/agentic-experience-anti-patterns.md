# Agentic Experience Anti-Patterns

Agentic experiences are new and easy to get wrong. These anti-patterns are common mistakes that hurt trust, usability, and adoption.

---

## 1. Magic Autonomy

The agent acts without any explanation, approval, or visibility.

**Why it fails**: Users do not know what happened or why. Mistakes erode trust.

**Better**: Show the plan, get approval for high-stakes actions, and log agent activity.

---

## 2. Anthropomorphism Overload

The agent is presented as a human with a name, personality, and emotions.

**Why it fails**: Users may over-trust, over-share, or blame the agent like a person.

**Better**: Present the AI as a tool. Use clear, helpful language without pretending to be human.

---

## 3. Black Box Reasoning

The agent gives an answer but no explanation or sources.

**Why it fails**: Users cannot verify, correct, or learn from the output.

**Better**: Show reasoning, cite sources, and highlight uncertainty.

---

## 4. Permission Creep

The agent asks for access to more data and capabilities over time, all at once.

**Why it fails**: Users lose control and privacy.

**Better**: Ask for specific permissions in context. Let users see and revoke access.

---

## 5. Over-Proactivity

The agent constantly interrupts with suggestions and actions.

**Why it fails**: It becomes noise. Users disable or ignore the agent.

**Better**: Be proactive only when high-confidence and high-value. Make suggestions easy to dismiss.

---

## 6. False Confidence

The agent always sounds certain, even when wrong.

**Why it fails**: Users trust wrong answers and make bad decisions.

**Better**: Communicate uncertainty. Phrase low-confidence output as tentative.

---

## 7. No Fallback

If the agent fails, there is no way to complete the task manually.

**Why it fails**: Users are stuck when the AI is down or wrong.

**Better**: Always provide a manual path. Preserve user progress on failure.

---

## 8. Hidden Limits

The agent appears unlimited but has unstated constraints.

**Why it fails**: Users hit walls unexpectedly.

**Better**: State capabilities and limits clearly and in context.

---

## 9. One-Shot Expectation

The agent expects a perfect prompt on the first try.

**Why it fails**: Users do not know how to phrase requests. They give up.

**Better**: Provide examples, templates, and iterative refinement.

---

## 10. Eternal Conversations

The agent expects a long, open-ended chat with no clear structure or end.

**Why it fails**: Users lose track of the task. They waste time.

**Better**: Provide structure: goals, steps, progress, and completion.

---

## 11. Agent Theater

The product shows fake “thinking” or simulated agent steps to look impressive.

**Why it fails**: It is dishonest and eventually obvious.

**Better**: Show real status and progress. Do not fabricate steps.

---

## 12. Feedback Black Hole

The agent collects feedback but users never see the result.

**Why it fails**: Users stop giving feedback.

**Better**: Acknowledge feedback, explain how it helps, and show improvements.

---

## 13. Over-Delegation

The agent handles too much without human oversight.

**Why it fails**: Errors have real consequences, and users lose agency.

**Better**: Match autonomy to risk. Require approval for high-stakes actions.

---

## 14. Summary

Avoid these patterns:

- Magic autonomy.
- Anthropomorphism overload.
- Black box reasoning.
- Permission creep.
- Over-proactivity.
- False confidence.
- No fallback.
- Hidden limits.
- One-shot expectation.
- Eternal conversations.
- Agent theater.
- Feedback black hole.
- Over-delegation.

---

## 15. Checklist

- [ ] Agent actions are visible and explainable.
- [ ] High-stakes actions require approval.
- [ ] AI is presented as a tool, not a person.
- [ ] Reasoning and sources are shown.
- [ ] Permissions are asked in context and can be revoked.
- [ ] Proactivity is restrained and valuable.
- [ ] Uncertainty is communicated.
- [ ] Manual fallbacks exist.
- [ ] Capabilities and limits are clear.
- [ ] Users get examples and can iterate.
- [ ] Conversations have structure and goals.
- [ ] Feedback leads to visible improvement.
- [ ] Autonomy matches the risk of the task.
