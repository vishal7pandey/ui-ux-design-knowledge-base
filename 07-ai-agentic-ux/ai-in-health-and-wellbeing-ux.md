# AI in Health and Wellbeing UX

AI in health and wellbeing affects people’s bodies, minds, and safety. The UX must be careful, compassionate, and evidence-based.

---

## 1. Key Considerations

### Safety

- Health advice can have serious consequences.
- AI should not replace professional care.
- Disclose limitations clearly.

### Privacy

- Health data is sensitive.
- Strong consent and security are required.
- Follow HIPAA, GDPR, and other regulations.

### Trust

- Users need to trust the AI with personal health data.
- Evidence, sources, and transparency build trust.

### Empathy

- Health issues can be stressful.
- Use calm, supportive language.
- Avoid fear or judgment.

---

## 2. Health AI UX Principles

### Do not diagnose

- The AI can provide information, track trends, and suggest next steps.
- It should not give definitive diagnoses or prescriptions.
- Direct users to qualified professionals for diagnosis and treatment.

### Be evidence-based

- Provide information from reliable sources.
- Cite medical guidelines or research where appropriate.
- Avoid unproven claims.

### Personal but not invasive

- Personalize based on user data.
- Do not pry or make assumptions.
- Let users control what they share.

### Calm and clear

- Use plain language.
- Avoid alarmist language.
- Present risk in understandable terms.

---

## 3. Common Use Cases

- Symptom checkers and triage.
- Medication reminders.
- Mental health chatbots.
- Fitness coaching.
- Sleep tracking and advice.
- Chronic disease management.
- Nutrition guidance.

---

## 4. UX Patterns

### Triage and symptom checking

- Ask clear, step-by-step questions.
- Provide a recommendation, not a diagnosis.
- Recommend when to seek professional care.

### Tracking and trends

- Show data over time.
- Highlight patterns and changes.
- Let users add context and notes.

### Coaching and nudges

- Encourage healthy behavior.
- Avoid shaming or pressure.
- Personalize frequency and tone.

### Mental health support

- Use empathetic, non-judgmental language.
- Provide crisis resources.
- Do not pretend to be a therapist.

### Emergency guidance

- If a user may be in danger, direct them to emergency services.
- Do not downplay serious symptoms.

---

## 5. Disclaimers and Safety

- “This is not medical advice.”
- “Consult a healthcare professional for diagnosis and treatment.”
- “If you are experiencing an emergency, call your local emergency number.”
- “This app is not a replacement for a doctor.”

---

## 6. Best Practices

- Work with medical professionals and ethicists.
- Follow health regulations and standards.
- Test with diverse users, including those with disabilities.
- Be transparent about what the AI can and cannot do.
- Provide clear escalation to human care.
- Protect health data with strong security.
- Use inclusive, non-stigmatizing language.
- Monitor for harm and misinformation.

---

## 7. Common Mistakes

- Giving diagnosis or treatment advice.
- Downplaying serious symptoms.
- Using fear-based messaging.
- Not providing professional escalation.
- Collecting health data without clear consent.
- Making unproven claims.
- Stigmatizing conditions or users.
- Over-automating mental health care.

---

## 8. Checklist

- [ ] AI does not diagnose or prescribe.
- [ ] Disclaimers are clear and prominent.
- [ ] Information is evidence-based and sourced.
- [ ] Users are directed to professional care when needed.
- [ ] Privacy and consent meet health regulations.
- [ ] Language is calm, empathetic, and non-judgmental.
- [ ] Emergency guidance is available.
- [ ] Personalization respects user control.
- [ ] Crisis and escalation paths are clear.
- [ ] The product is reviewed by medical and ethical experts.
- [ ] Accessibility and inclusivity are addressed.
