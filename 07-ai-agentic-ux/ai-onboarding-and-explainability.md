# AI Onboarding and Explainability

AI features need their own onboarding and explanation. Users must understand what the AI can do, how to interact with it, and why it behaves the way it does.

---

## 1. AI Onboarding

### Set expectations

Users need to know:

- What the AI can do.
- What it cannot do.
- How to get started.
- How to give feedback.

### Show the value

- Help users reach a quick win with the AI.
- Provide example prompts or commands.
- Demonstrate a useful, low-risk task.

### Build confidence

- Start with safe, reversible actions.
- Show that the user is in control.
- Offer suggestions, not demands.

---

## 2. Onboarding Patterns

### Example prompts

Show 3–5 suggested queries the user can run.

```
Try: “Summarize this document.”
Try: “What are the next steps from this meeting?”
Try: “Draft a reply to this email.”
```

### Guided first task

Walk the user through one complete AI interaction.

- Explain the input.
- Show how to review the output.
- Show how to edit or reject.
- Show how to give feedback.

### Tooltips and coach marks

- Highlight the AI feature on first use.
- Explain how to invoke it.
- Keep tooltips short and dismissible.

### Sandbox or demo

- Let users try the AI without real consequences.
- Provide sample content and scenarios.

---

## 3. Explainability

Users should be able to understand AI output and behavior.

### Show what the AI is doing

- “Summarizing 12 emails…”
- “Searching your documents…”
- “Drafting a response…”

### Show why it produced the result

- Cite sources.
- Show the data or reasoning.
- Highlight the parts of input that influenced the output.

### Show confidence

- “High confidence” / “Low confidence.”
- “This answer is based on 3 sources.”
- “I’m not sure about this part.”

### Allow inspection

- Expand to see the model’s steps.
- View source material.
- See alternative suggestions.

---

## 4. Explainability Patterns

### Source citations

- Link each part of the output to the source document.
- Show excerpts or references.

### Reasoning steps

- Break down how the AI reached a conclusion.
- Show intermediate steps: search, compare, synthesize.

### Confidence scores

- Indicate how certain the AI is.
- Ask the user to verify low-confidence outputs.

### Highlighting

- Highlight the parts of a user’s input that influenced the response.
- Show which words or phrases mattered most.

### Model cards or info panels

- Explain the model’s known limitations.
- Show training data or intended use.
- Provide links to learn more.

---

## 5. Best Practices

- Be honest about capabilities and limits.
- Do not anthropomorphize the AI.
- Use plain language, not marketing copy.
- Provide ways to verify, edit, and undo.
- Make feedback easy.
- Explain errors and failures.
- Let users opt out or disable AI features.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Overselling what the AI can do.
- Hiding that a feature is AI-powered.
- No way to see sources or reasoning.
- Overly confident language for uncertain output.
- No onboarding for AI-specific features.
- Blaming users for AI mistakes.
- Not explaining why an AI output is wrong.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] AI capabilities and limits are clearly stated.
- [ ] Onboarding provides a quick, safe first task.
- [ ] Example prompts help users get started.
- [ ] Users know how to give feedback.
- [ ] AI output is attributable and verifiable.
- [ ] Confidence or uncertainty is shown.
- [ ] Sources and reasoning are inspectable.
- [ ] Errors are explained, not hidden.
- [ ] Users can edit, undo, or disable AI features.
- [ ] Language is honest and not anthropomorphizing.
