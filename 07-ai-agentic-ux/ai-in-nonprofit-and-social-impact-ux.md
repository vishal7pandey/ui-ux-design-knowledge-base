# AI in Nonprofit and Social Impact UX

AI in the social sector can help nonprofits serve more people, raise funds, and measure impact. The UX must be ethical, inclusive, and focused on the mission.

---

## 1. Key Principles

### Mission-driven

- Technology should serve the organization’s goals and the people they help.
- Avoid tech for tech’s sake.

### Inclusion

- Work for the communities being served.
- Support low connectivity, low literacy, and local languages.

### Ethics

- Do no harm.
- Protect vulnerable populations.
- Avoid surveillance or exploitation.

### Sustainability

- Tools should be affordable and maintainable.
- Consider long-term capacity and training.

### Impact

- Focus on measurable outcomes.
- Use data to learn and improve.

---

## 2. Common Use Cases

- Beneficiary intake and matching.
- Program recommendations.
- Donor and volunteer management.
- Impact measurement and reporting.
- Case management.
- Fundraising and outreach.
- Translation and communication.
- Resource allocation.
- Crisis response.

---

## 3. UX Patterns

### Beneficiary intake

- Simple, respectful forms.
- Multi-language support.
- Minimal data collection.
- Clear consent.

### Service matching

- Match people to programs and services.
- Explain the match.
- Let caseworkers review and adjust.

### Case management

- Track progress and interventions.
- Flag high-priority cases.
- Support notes and collaboration.

### Impact dashboard

- Show outcomes and trends.
- Use clear, non-technical language.
- Link to stories and qualitative data.

### Donor and volunteer portal

- Show impact of contributions.
- Recommend ways to help.
- Keep communication personal and transparent.

### Crisis response

- Rapid needs assessment.
- Resource mapping and dispatch.
- Real-time coordination.

### Translation and outreach

- Translate materials into local languages.
- Adapt messaging to different communities.
- Respect cultural context.

---

## 4. Best Practices

- Design with the community, not for it.
- Keep tools simple and affordable.
- Protect beneficiary privacy and dignity.
- Train staff and volunteers.
- Measure outcomes, not just outputs.
- Support low-tech and offline use.
- Avoid paternalistic or surveillance approaches.
- Provide clear consent and data use policies.
- Build for local context and language.
- Share learning and impact openly.

---

## 5. Ethics and Inclusion

- Do not collect more data than needed.
- Avoid using AI for gatekeeping without oversight.
- Include affected communities in design and review.
- Be transparent about how AI informs decisions.
- Provide recourse for errors.
- Ensure accessibility for all users.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Imposing technology without community input.
- Collecting sensitive data without consent.
- Over-complicating tools for under-resourced staff.
- Using AI to exclude or sort people unfairly.
- Not measuring real impact.
- Ignoring connectivity and device constraints.
- No training or support.
- Surveillance under the guise of impact.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Tools serve the mission and the community.
- [ ] Affected communities are involved in design.
- [ ] Intake and matching are respectful and transparent.
- [ ] Data collection is minimal and consented.
- [ ] Tools are simple, affordable, and maintainable.
- [ ] Offline and low-connectivity use is supported.
- [ ] Multi-language and cultural adaptation are provided.
- [ ] Staff and volunteers are trained.
- [ ] Impact is measured and shared.
- [ ] Privacy and dignity are protected.
- [ ] Recourse and review are available.
- [ ] The product is tested in real program contexts.
