# UX Writing for AI and Agentic Products

Writing for AI products requires clarity, honesty, and care. Users need to understand what the AI is doing, what it can do, and where the boundaries are.

---

## 1. Core Principles

### Be honest

Do not overstate what the AI can do. Do not pretend it is human.

### Be clear

Use plain language. Avoid jargon like “generative,” “LLM,” or “tokens” unless the audience knows them.

### Be specific

Say what the AI does and what it does not do. Vague claims create false trust.

### Be helpful

Guide users toward success. Show examples and next steps.

### Be transparent

Explain when the AI is uncertain, wrong, or limited.

---

## 2. AI-Specific Writing Patterns

### Capability framing

- “I can summarize documents, answer questions, and draft replies.”
- “I cannot access the internet or real-time data.”

### Limitation disclosure

- “I may make mistakes. Please verify important information.”
- “I do not know about events after March 2025.”

### Action descriptions

- “Summarizing 3 documents…”
- “Searching your calendar…”
- “Drafting a response…”

### Confidence and uncertainty

- “I’m not sure about this part.”
- “This is based on 2 sources.”
- “I may be wrong here.”

### Suggestions

- “Try asking: ‘What are the next steps?’”
- “You can also say: ‘Make this shorter.’”

---

## 3. Avoid These Phrases

| Avoid | Use instead |
|-------|-------------|
| “I feel…” | “I found…” or “I generated…” |
| “I think…” | “It appears…” or “This suggests…” |
| “I’m sorry, I can’t…” | “I can’t [do that]. I can [do this].” |
| “As an AI…” | Remove or rephrase. |
| “You’re right / wrong” | Avoid assigning right or wrong to the user. |

---

## 4. Tone

- Keep the tone professional but friendly.
- Match the product’s voice.
- Do not be overly apologetic or servile.
- Be calm and factual when the AI fails.
- Use active, direct language.

---

## 5. Microcopy for AI States

### Loading

- “Analyzing your document…”
- “This may take a moment.”

### Error

- “I couldn’t complete that. Here is what you can try: …”
- “There was a problem. Your data is safe.”

### Empty / no results

- “I couldn’t find anything about that. Try rephrasing or using a broader term.”
- “No matches found. Here are some things you can ask.”

### Success

- “Done. Your summary is ready.”
- “Saved to your notes.”

---

## 6. Onboarding Copy

- “What this assistant can do for you: …”
- “Examples of what you can ask: …”
- “How to get the best results: …”

---

## 7. Best Practices

- Show value and limits in the first screen.
- Use example prompts to guide users.
- Keep AI references natural: “your AI assistant” not “the artificial intelligence.”
- Make it easy to report mistakes.
- Use consistent terms for AI features.
- Do not anthropomorphize.
- Test copy with real users.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

- Promising more than the AI can do.
- Using overly technical language.
- Writing as if the AI has feelings.
- No disclosure of uncertainty.
- Unclear error messages.
- Inconsistent names for the AI.
- Copy that sounds like marketing, not guidance.

---

## 9. Checklist

- [ ] Capabilities and limits are stated in plain language.
- [ ] AI is described as a tool, not a person.
- [ ] Loading, error, and success states have clear copy.
- [ ] Uncertainty and confidence are communicated.
- [ ] Example prompts guide users.
- [ ] Limitations are specific and honest.
- [ ] Error messages are helpful and calm.
- [ ] AI names and feature labels are consistent.
- [ ] Copy is tested with real users.
- [ ] Microcopy respects privacy and trust.
