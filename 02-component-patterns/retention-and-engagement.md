# Retention and Engagement

Retention is whether users keep coming back. Engagement is how actively they use the product. Both are driven by value, habit, and a smooth experience.

---

## 1. Retention Fundamentals

Retention depends on:

- **Value**: the product solves a real problem.
- **Habit**: users have a reason to return.
- **Trust**: the product is reliable and respectful.
- **Progress**: users feel they are getting better over time.

---

## 2. Retention Metrics

| Metric | What it measures |
|--------|------------------|
| **Day 1 / 7 / 30 retention** | Users who return after signup. |
| **Cohort retention** | Retention of users grouped by start date. |
| **Churn** | Users who stop using the product. |
| **Session frequency** | How often users return. |
| **Core action frequency** | How often users do the main thing. |

---

## 3. Engagement Patterns

### Habit loops

Trigger → Action → Reward

- **External trigger**: notification, email, reminder.
- **Internal trigger**: a thought or feeling the user has.
- **Action**: the user does something simple.
- **Reward**: the user gets value or satisfaction.

Make the action easy and the reward meaningful.

### Progress and streaks

- Show streaks for repeated behavior.
- Celebrate milestones.
- Use progress bars and completion percentages.

### Notifications

- Send relevant, timely, and actionable notifications.
- Personalize based on user behavior.
- Allow users to control frequency and channels.

### Personalization

- Surface relevant content based on usage.
- Adapt the home screen or dashboard to the user’s role.
- Recommend next actions.

### Social proof and community

- Show what others are doing.
- Add collaboration, sharing, and leaderboard features.
- Build a sense of belonging.

---

## 4. Re-engagement

When users become inactive:

- **Email or push reminders** with a clear value proposition.
- **Feature announcements** for new capabilities.
- **Win-back offers** for paid users.
- **Simplified flows** to lower the barrier to return.

Avoid spam. Each re-engagement message must earn the user’s attention.

---

## 5. Reducing Friction

Friction kills retention. Audit the product for:

- Slow loading or crashes.
- Confusing navigation.
- Unnecessary steps to complete core actions.
- Hidden or complex settings.
- Poor error handling.
- Missing offline or low-bandwidth support.

---

## 6. Trust and Delight

- Be reliable. If a feature works, it should work every time.
- Keep promises. Do not over-promise and under-deliver.
- Add small moments of delight: success messages, celebrations, helpful tips.
- Respect the user’s time, data, and attention.

---

## 7. Feedback Loops

- Ask users for feedback at the right moments.
- Close the loop by showing how feedback led to change.
- Monitor support tickets and reviews for patterns.
- Make users feel heard.

---

## 8. Common Mistakes

- Sending too many notifications.
- Focusing on new users and ignoring existing ones.
- Adding features without improving core value.
- Measuring vanity metrics like downloads or signups.
- Not addressing churn reasons.
- Over-optimizing for engagement at the cost of trust.

---

## 9. Checklist

- [ ] Core value is clear and easy to reach.
- [ ] Activation and retention are measured by cohort.
- [ ] Habit loops are designed around real user needs.
- [ ] Progress, streaks, and milestones are visible.
- [ ] Notifications are relevant and controllable.
- [ ] Personalization is based on behavior, not assumptions.
- [ ] Re-engagement is respectful and valuable.
- [ ] Friction is regularly audited and removed.
- [ ] Feedback loops are in place.
- [ ] Churn reasons are researched and addressed.
