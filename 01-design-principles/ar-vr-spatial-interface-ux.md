# AR/VR Spatial Interface UX

Augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) place interfaces in 3D space. Designing for spatial UX means rethinking layout, input, comfort, and context.

---

## 1. Spatial Design Principles

### Comfort

- Avoid making users hold their arms up for long periods.
- Keep content within a natural field of view.
- Minimize fast motion and vection.
- Respect resting positions.

### Clarity

- Use depth, scale, and position to show hierarchy.
- Keep text large and legible.
- Avoid clutter in 3D space.

### Context

- AR: design for the real environment.
- VR: design for a fully virtual space.
- Both: consider safety, physical space, and other people.

---

## 2. Field of View

- Place important content within the central 60 degrees.
- Avoid placing critical UI at the edges.
- Allow head movement; do not lock content to the screen center.
- Provide gentle audio or visual cues for off-screen content.

---

## 3. Input Methods

### Gaze

- Look at an object to select.
- Use dwell or a confirmation to avoid accidental selection.

### Hand gestures

- Use simple, comfortable gestures.
- Provide visual feedback for gesture recognition.
- Avoid gestures that require holding the arm up.

### Controllers

- Map buttons consistently.
- Use haptic feedback for confirmation.
- Show virtual hands or pointers.

### Voice

- Useful for hands-free tasks.
- Confirm commands to avoid mistakes.
- Support quiet or noisy environments.

---

## 4. Distance and Scale

- Place text and UI at a comfortable distance (usually 1–3 meters).
- Make text and controls large enough to read from a distance.
- Use depth to show hierarchy: closer is more important.
- Scale content to the user’s environment.

---

## 5. Safety and Environment

### AR

- Warn users about nearby obstacles.
- Avoid placing content where it blocks real hazards.
- Design for varied lighting and surfaces.

### VR

- Provide a safe boundary or guardian system.
- Let users pause and exit quickly.
- Warn about motion sickness.
- Support seated and standing experiences.

---

## 6. UI Patterns for Spatial Interfaces

### Floating panels

- 2D panels placed in 3D space.
- Use for menus, settings, and information.
- Keep panels at a comfortable angle and distance.

### World-locked content

- Content attached to a fixed point in the real or virtual world.
- Good for annotations, objects, and persistent info.

### Head-locked content

- UI that follows the user’s view.
- Use sparingly for HUD-like elements.
- Avoid nausea by keeping it minimal.

### Direct manipulation

- Grabbing, moving, and resizing virtual objects.
- Use visual and haptic feedback.

### Teleportation and locomotion

- Let users move through space without physical walking.
- Provide options for comfort and accessibility.

---

## 7. Performance

- Maintain high frame rates (at least 72 FPS for VR).
- Reduce latency for head and hand tracking.
- Optimize textures, geometry, and effects.
- Avoid sudden frame drops.

---

## 8. Accessibility

- Support multiple input methods.
- Provide seated modes.
- Allow users to adjust text size and contrast.
- Offer comfort settings for motion and intensity.
- Respect reduced motion preferences.

---

## 9. Common Mistakes

- Small text and tiny controls.
- Content placed too close or too far.
- Requiring long, tiring gestures.
- Ignoring the physical environment.
- Fast or disorienting motion.
- Overloading the field of view.
- Not providing a quick exit.
- Ignoring motion sickness.

---

## 10. Checklist

- [ ] Important content is in the central field of view.
- [ ] UI is at a comfortable distance and scale.
- [ ] Input methods are comfortable and accessible.
- [ ] Depth and scale show hierarchy.
- [ ] AR designs respect the physical environment.
- [ ] VR designs include safety boundaries and comfort options.
- [ ] Frame rate and latency are optimized.
- [ ] Motion is smooth and not disorienting.
- [ ] Users can quickly exit or pause.
- [ ] Multiple input methods are supported.
- [ ] Accessibility and comfort settings are provided.
