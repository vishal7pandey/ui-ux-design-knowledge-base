# Haptics and Sensory UX

Haptics and other sensory feedback add a physical layer to digital interactions. Used well, they confirm actions, provide feedback, and add richness. Used poorly, they annoy or confuse.

---

## 1. Haptics

Haptics are physical vibrations that provide touch feedback.

### When to use haptics

- Confirm a button press or selection.
- Signal success, completion, or error.
- Add emphasis to important events.
- Provide feedback in silent or eyes-free contexts.
- Enhance gaming, gestures, and immersive experiences.

### Types of haptic feedback

| Type | Use case |
|------|----------|
| **Light tap** | Subtle confirmation. |
| **Sharp click** | Button press. |
| **Success** | Task completed. |
| **Warning** | Error or invalid action. |
| **Pattern** | Notifications, alarms. |

### Best practices

- Use haptics sparingly.
- Match intensity to the action.
- Pair haptics with visual or audio feedback.
- Avoid haptics for every small action.
- Respect system-level haptic settings.
- Do not rely on haptics as the only feedback.

---

## 2. Sound and Audio Feedback

- Use sound for confirmation, alerts, and notifications.
- Keep sounds short and meaningful.
- Provide volume and mute controls.
- Avoid auto-playing sound unexpectedly.
- Respect silent mode and do-not-disturb.

---

## 3. Vibration Patterns

- Use consistent patterns for the same type of event.
- Do not use long, continuous vibrations.
- Differentiate success, warning, and error.
- Let users customize or disable vibration.

---

## 4. Multisensory Feedback

Combine touch, sound, and visual feedback for stronger signals.

- Example: a toggle switch uses a click sound, a haptic tap, and a color change.
- Avoid using all channels for minor actions.
- Use multiple channels for high-priority events.

---

## 5. Accessibility

- Do not rely on haptics or sound alone.
- Provide visual alternatives.
- Honor system settings for reduced motion and sound.
- Avoid flashing or pulsing that can trigger sensitivities.

---

## 6. Common Mistakes

- Haptic feedback on every tap.
- Inconsistent vibration patterns.
- Strong haptics for minor actions.
- Relying on sound in silent environments.
- No way to disable sensory feedback.
- Ignoring system preferences.

---

## 7. Checklist

- [ ] Haptics are purposeful and sparing.
- [ ] Intensity matches the action.
- [ ] Haptics are paired with visual or audio feedback.
- [ ] Patterns are consistent and meaningful.
- [ ] Users can disable or adjust sensory feedback.
- [ ] System settings are respected.
- [ ] Sensory feedback is not the only source of information.
- [ ] Accessibility alternatives are provided.
